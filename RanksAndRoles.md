# Holdfast Ranks & Roles

Rank represents trust. A billet represents responsibility.

Holdfast uses a simple distinction:

- **Rank** is enduring trust, judgment, and authority earned over time.
- **Rep** is the permanent record of useful service.
- **A billet** is a current job the guild needs done.
- **Service Marks** are spendable goodwill that members can use to ask the guild for help.

Rep creates eligibility. It never purchases rank.

## How Rep Relates to Rank

Rep only goes up. It is never spent and continues accumulating after every rank breakpoint.

The current service floors are:

| Rank | Rep floor | What it means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Corporal | 3,000 | The last rank primarily reached through contribution, assuming good standing. |
| Sergeant | 9,000 | Minimum service floor plus recommendation and demonstrated leadership. |
| Master Sergeant | 21,000 | Minimum service floor plus sustained NCO performance and development of others. |
| Sergeant Major | 42,000 | Minimum service floor plus exceptional senior trust, judgment, and mentorship. |

These are floors, not promises. A member can earn enormous lifetime Rep and remain a Corporal if they do not want formal leadership or if the organization does not need another leader.

Officer ranks are commissioned separately. No amount of Rep automatically creates a Lieutenant, Captain, or Major.

## The Rank Ladder

### Recruit

A new member learning the guild and finding their place.

### Private

A full member in good standing. No leadership expectation.

### Corporal

The first level of earned trust. Corporals may organize small activities, assist established leaders, and take initiative, but Corporal may also be a permanent rank.

### Sergeant

A proven small-group leader and mentor. Sergeant requires more than a Rep floor: it requires recommendation, sound judgment, and evidence that the member makes other people more capable.

### Master Sergeant

An experienced NCO who can run recurring activities, develop junior leaders, solve ordinary problems, and maintain standards.

### Sergeant Major

The senior enlisted rank. Sergeant Majors strengthen the NCO corps, preserve institutional knowledge, mentor leaders, and identify problems before they become failures.

### Lieutenant

The first commissioned rank. A Lieutenant is appointed when Holdfast needs another officer and trusts someone with broader authority.

### Captain

An experienced officer trusted with significant programs, responsibilities, or recurring operations.

### Major

A senior officer trusted with broad responsibility and cross-guild coordination.

### Commander

Holdfast’s unique guildmaster rank. The Commander protects the guild’s continuity and direction while delegating ordinary work.

## Current Billets

Billets are jobs, not permanent ownership of people. They can change hands as members step forward, take breaks, or find a better fit. Leaving a billet does not remove the rank or trust someone has earned.

### Steward

The Commander’s trusted deputy, able to keep Holdfast moving during an absence.

### Quartermaster

Responsible for the guild bank, materials, professions, crafting, and practical economic work.

### Raid Leader

Responsible for an assigned raid team or program, including schedules, rosters, preparation, and calls.

### PvP Lead

Responsible for organized guild PvP, including premades, response groups, events, and training.

## Service Marks

Rep records what you have given. Service Marks let you ask something back.

Members can earn Marks through approved guild work and later use them to request help with a difficult quest, dungeon, profession, item, or other approved need.

Marks never buy rank, authority, or someone’s respect.

The purpose of the structure is simple: make contribution visible, make responsibility clear, and keep leadership tied to trust rather than grinding.
